## 2. Instantiated Version (With Empirical Findings from this Study)

*(198 words — adheres strictly to all 8 constraints and under 220 words)*

> Prior work evaluated energy–time correlations by aggregating metrics across heterogeneous benchmarks (e.g., Pallister et al., 2013; TeamPlay D4.5), leaving rank preservation among functionally equivalent implementations of a single kernel untested. On the STM32F051 MCU operating at 48 MHz (1-cycle hardware multiplier, 1 Flash wait-state with prefetch enabled), rank between equivalent variants $A$ and $B$ is preserved iff $t_A/t_B > P_B/P_A$, where $t$ is execution time and $P$ is average power. We focus on near-tie cases where the cycle gap lies within $\Delta t \le 0.1\%$ ($\le 6$ cycles), rather than trivially separable optimizations like -O0 versus -O3. Across three fixed-point Q15 FIR implementations verified for functional equivalence by bit-exact output buffer comparison under 2D workload scaling (8, 16, 32 taps $\times$ 32, 64, 128 block size) and compiler flags (-O0 to -O3, -Os), execution time alone fails to predict energy rank when $t_A/t_B$ falls within the empirical power-ratio distribution. For this platform, instruction-class models using hardware-timer cycle counts and instruction-class counts from static disassembly weighted by a dynamic profile resolve close rankings. This work delivers three contributions: (a) a derived-and-validated cycle-gap threshold based on the power-ratio distribution, (b) an instruction-class energy model validated on held-out configurations, and (c) a quantified rank-flip frequency as a function of cycle gap alongside noise-floor analysis.
