# Intermittent streaming FIR: candidate-gap screen

Date: 20 October 2026. Research only; this does not select or implement a new project scope.

## Recommendation

**No-go for “crash-consistent FIR with adaptive blocks/checkpoints” as the original contribution.** Existing work already covers atomic persistent tasks, energy-dependent checkpoint suppression, task coalescing/splitting, resumable convolution loops, and even energy-calibrated FIR accelerator tiles. A narrower question about **which FIR state to retain versus reconstruct from a durable input log, under a bounded backlog and output-latency contract**, deserves a small rejection probe, but remains a hypothesis rather than an established literature gap. This conclusion is an inference from the primary sources below, not proof that no equivalent technique exists.

This is a substantial systems-scope expansion from an exact Q15 Cortex-M0 arithmetic harness. It introduces persistence, recovery, admission and output protocols, a memory fault model, and energy accounting. Publication being optional makes a careful experimental college paper feasible; it does not turn an application of known checkpoint frameworks into a new method.

## Nearest work and what it closes

| Primary source | Relevant established result | Implication for this candidate |
|---|---|---|
| [Chain, OOPSLA 2016, §§2–4](https://brandonlucia.com/pubs/chain.pdf) | Restartable tasks communicate through separated channels, preserving consistent task-level progress. Its I/O discussion explicitly warns that re-executed sensor reads may differ and physical actuator effects cannot be undone. | Transactional FIR history updates and acquisition isolation are applications of an existing model; channel atomicity alone does not establish exactly-once external output. |
| [Alpaca, OOPSLA 2017, §§3–5](https://abstract.ece.cmu.edu/pubs/alpaca.pdf) | Privatizes shared updates and commits successful tasks; requires tasks to fit the energy buffer. Describes input-dependent idempotence problems and a dedicated acquisition-task pattern. | Double-buffering state/output and committing a cursor are baseline engineering. A large FIR call that repeatedly restarts is a known granularity failure. |
| [Chinchilla, OSDI 2018, §§3–4](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi18-maeng.pdf) | Overprovisions potential checkpoints and dynamically suppresses unnecessary ones according to observed progress; includes consistency protection and a non-termination check. The introductory example is a 1-D convolution. | “Adapt checkpoint interval to available energy” is already occupied, including convolution motivation. |
| [SONIC/TAILS, ASPLOS 2019, §§6–7](https://g-ram.github.io/files/sonic_asplos_2019.pdf) | SONIC persists loop progress and uses loop-ordered double-buffering for idempotent convolution iterations. TAILS accelerates with LEA FIR discrete-time convolution. Its one-time calibration chooses DMA/vector tile sizes, halves an unsuccessful size, and retains a size that completes within the energy buffer. | Partial FIR/convolution progress and energy-feasible block selection are close prior art, despite its DNN workload rather than an unbounded sensor stream. Replacing one-time calibration with repeated adaptation is insufficient by itself. |
| [Coala, ACM TOSN 2020, §§3–5](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_3355f89a-63cf-4a0d-a70c-9b75e3e140ed) | Dynamically coalesces tasks to amortize commits and splits overlarge tasks; recent execution history guides adaptation, and memory management handles dependencies introduced by coalescing. | Generic adaptive batching plus crash safety is already a contribution of another system. |
| [Samoyed, PLDI 2019, §§2–3](https://kiwanmaeng.com/papers/pldi2019-samoyed.pdf) | Supports peripheral atomic functions with JIT checkpoints, logging, profiling, workload scaling, and software fallback. Discusses inconsistent audio sampling intervals across outages; evaluates LEA convolution. | Sensor timing and accelerator recovery are not fresh problems. A stream contract must address physical capture separately from arithmetic recovery. |
| [Surbatovich et al., OOPSLA 2020, publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1145/3428231) | Formalizes memory/input correctness and repeated-input-operation problems, and provides an input-correct runtime algorithm. | “Correct after reboot” needs explicit input semantics, not only equality of final RAM contents. |

Library boundary: [Arm CMSIS-DSP FIR documentation](https://arm-software.github.io/CMSIS-DSP/main/group__FIR.html) describes block processing, mutable state updated after a block, and the usual `numTaps + blockSize - 1` state allocation, with implementation-specific exceptions. It documents arithmetic/state APIs, not a transactional persistence protocol. [TI DSPLib real FIR documentation](https://software-dl.ti.com/msp430/msp430_public_sw/mcu/msp430/DSPLib/latest/exports/html/group__dsplib__filter__real__fir.html) exposes LEA FIR and placement/alignment requirements. Neither should be treated as a crash-safe baseline without an explicit wrapper.

## Required setting and exactness contract

The following are **proposed assumptions**, not properties of the existing harness or an ordinary Cortex-M0 board:

1. A finite or rate-controlled source supplies immutable `(sequence number, Q15 sample)` records. A record becomes *admitted* only after a crash-safe durable append. Either the source can replay unacknowledged records or capture has its own powered/persistent buffer. A preloaded trace is the simplest simulator case, but models processing a stored stream, not continuous sensing through outages.
2. Coefficients and initial history are fixed. For every admitted index `n`, the reference is the existing exact Q15 FIR arithmetic over the same ordered stream, including its accumulator, shift, saturation, zero-history and tail rules. No dropping, interpolation or restart of history is permitted.
3. Recovery atomically identifies a consistent input cursor, output cursor, history version, and any retained partial-work descriptor. Partial-work state must specify output/tap indices and accumulator precision; changing accumulation or saturation order must preserve the reference result. Torn-write behavior, atomic persistence width, write ordering and reboot behavior are specified in the model.
4. “Produced” means committed to a local durable output log keyed by sample index. External delivery uses an acknowledged receiver with durable duplicate suppression, or a genuinely transactional sink. A send followed by a local “sent” flag is insufficient: a crash between them leaves ambiguous delivery. This distinction follows established replay/checkpoint and sink requirements in [Flink's official stateful-processing model](https://github.com/apache/flink/blob/master/docs/content/docs/concepts/stateful-stream-processing.md) and [Google Dataflow's exactly-once documentation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/dataflow/docs/concepts/exactly-once).
5. Liveness is conditional: eventually there must be enough energy to finish the smallest recovery/commit unit, and sufficient long-run processing service for the admitted input rate. Backlog capacity and overflow behavior are part of the contract; unbounded “eventually exact” output with finite storage is not a free guarantee.

**Impossibility boundary (deduction):** if an unrecorded input sample disappears during an outage, two physical input sequences differing only in that sample leave identical recoverable device state. For a nonzero relevant FIR coefficient, they can require different outputs. No recovery algorithm can output both exact answers. Likewise, a receiver that cannot distinguish duplicate sends cannot make an ambiguous external side effect exactly once merely because the MCU's history is consistent. Claim exactness for admitted durable records only, or explicitly add capture/replay and receiver support. Samoyed's sampling example and Chain's I/O limitations independently motivate these boundaries; they do not solve missing observations.

## Is anything FIR-specific left?

**Possible research question, not a novelty claim:** given a durable input log already needed for admission, can a FIR-specific recovery policy exploit its finite input history to choose among (a) retaining the last `T−1` samples/history, (b) reconstructing that history from retained input records, and (c) retaining partial tap/output work, while satisfying a fixed backlog and latency bound? The hypothesis is that jointly choosing *what* persists and *how much* work commits could outperform generic task scheduling at equal semantics and storage budget.

The FIR structure here is an inference from the library's history representation: history can be recovered from input, whereas a generic stateful computation may need replaying its transition function. However, retaining a ring-buffer cursor into an already persistent input window may make the supposed history-saving problem disappear. FIR outputs also have no output feedback; independent output recomputation can be simpler than persisting partial sums. Those are reasons to reject, not conceal, a weak contribution. The inspected sources do not establish that this joint optimization is unresolved; finite-window streaming, application-aware checkpointing and persistent-memory recovery literature would need a further focused novelty search before selection.

## Strongest baseline and minimal decisive probe

### More promising bounded empirical question: realization × recovery

A sharper exploratory title is **“When does intermittent persistence reverse the ranking of exact FIR realizations?”** Compare complete direct, symmetry-folded direct, and transposed implementations under the same per-sample output commit semantics, rather than inventing a generic checkpoint scheduler. MathWorks' primary documentation distinguishes [direct/symmetric state and fixed-point structures](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simulink/slref/discretefirfilter.html) and [transposed FIR state precision](https://www.mathworks.com/help/dsp/ref/dfilt.dffirt.html). This matters because mathematical transfer-function equivalence alone does not establish identical quantized outputs. [Arm's Q15 reference](https://arm-software.github.io/CMSIS-DSP/v1.17.1/group__FIR.html) distinguishes full 64-bit accumulation from its faster wrapping accumulator alternative.

**Derived model, to verify before measurement:** for `T` taps with Q15 inputs, direct/folded input history is `2(T−1)` bytes, while a straightforward transposed realization retaining each partial sum in 64 bits has `8(T−1)` bytes of state. This is an implementation choice for exactness, not a universal lower bound: coefficient-specific range proofs could permit smaller exact states. A direct persistent ring need overwrite only one 16-bit sample plus metadata per input; it need not snapshot its entire history. Transposed per-sample in-place persistence changes up to `T−1` wide states, introducing a multiwrite transaction or an undo/redo strategy. Conversely, volatile transposed work plus occasional snapshots can amortize writes, but loses/reconstructs work on reboot. A symmetric direct realization shares the input-history footprint and can reduce multiplies when coefficients are *exactly* symmetric; its pre-add must preserve the extra sum bit. These consequences follow by counting the specified representations and update equations, not from measured speedups.

Because direct history is already available as a window into the durable input ring, state-versus-replay is asymmetric: direct recovery can reset a cursor, whereas reconstructing transposed partial sums from recent input needs arithmetic (a straightforward triangular reconstruction uses at most `T(T−1)/2` products). A fair baseline may simply recover in direct form and switch to transposed only if later work repays reconstruction. Do not compare transposed against a direct implementation that needlessly copies full history every sample. None of the inspected SONIC/TAILS or Coala sections answers this complete-realization comparison under a durable per-sample stream contract; this is an **unanswered question in this source set**, not a confirmed global gap. An original empirical contribution could be a reproducible crossover map and explanation, including a negative finding, provided it goes beyond the already obvious wide-state write penalty.

For realizations `A` and `B`, evaluate per committed output

`J_R = compute_R + nvread_R + nvwrite_R + transaction_R + failed_work_R + restore_R`.

All terms are measured modeled energy or cycles consistently, and recovery terms include their observed frequency. A proposed break-even gate for a faster-compute but larger-state form `B` is

`compute_A − compute_B > (nvread_B−nvread_A) + (nvwrite_B−nvwrite_A) + (transaction_B−transaction_A) + (failed_work_B−failed_work_A) + (restore_B−restore_A)`.

The equality defines the crossover; an uncalibrated constant “energy per instruction” is insufficient when memory technologies differ. With a snapshot policy, a useful local gate is `writes_saved × cost_per_write > extra_replay_compute + extra_replay_reads + extra_metadata + extra_failed_work`; this is accounting, not a new theorem. The decisive result would be a ranking reversal relative to continuous execution over a nontrivial, independently justified parameter interval, surviving equal commit semantics, tuned baselines, and exact-state-width/range sensitivity. Reject this empirical direction if folded/direct dominates everywhere credible, the crossover is merely an arbitrary write-cost threshold with no deeper recovery interaction, or the result relies on coarse block-visible output while advertising per-sample commits. If a 10% practical-effect gate is chosen before the probe, require that margin beyond model uncertainty on held-out traces; 10% is a project decision, not a significance guarantee.

For the first probe, hold output visibility at one committed sample and vary only realization, retained state, and crash spacing. This isolates the question. Adaptive block/checkpoint selection should be added only if a meaningful crossover survives; if batching delays visibility, that is a separate, explicitly relaxed contract. Include two NVM models only if they are justified independently: a byte/word-addressable persistent RAM model and a flash model charging erase/program granularity, latency, durability ordering and write amplification. Label both hypothetical until a target part and primary datasheet support parameters. A Cortex-M0 instruction simulator by itself supplies none of these persistence guarantees.

Use the same exact FIR, durable source/sink contract, memory capacity and primitive operation costs for every policy. The strongest practical baseline is **not** a deliberately unsafe FIR or a single poorly chosen block size. Use a durable input ring with cursor-based history, atomic versioned output/cursor commit, and SONIC-style idempotent output/tap continuation; combine it with Coala/Chinchilla-style online granularity adaptation. Also sweep all feasible fixed commit/tile sizes and report the best fixed size per trace. A simulator port is a *reimplementation of described mechanisms*, not a measured port of those published MSP430 systems. Accelerator TAILS is a related-work bound, not an executable Cortex-M0 baseline.

Proposed smallest probe, before building a full runtime:

- Model a short Q15 stream with two tap counts, one tight and one generous persistence budget, and fixed, alternating-short/long, and abruptly changing execution-energy windows. Keep arrival and recharge timing separate from CPU energy; fixed full-capacitor restarts need not imply varying energy per boot. Charge all metadata, logs, replay, failed work, restore and commits.
- Exhaustively inject one crash at every persistence/compute boundary in a tiny instance, including mid-commit, then inject repeated crashes and sequences of short windows. Compare indexed durable outputs bit-for-bit with the uninterrupted reference; assert no missing/duplicate committed indices and no premature input reclamation. State exactly which atomic memory boundary is exhaustive.
- Compare retained-history, input-window cursor/reconstruction, partial-work continuation and the candidate joint policy. Report persistent bytes, bytes written, work repeated, committed outputs per modeled energy, maximum backlog and tail latency. Use a small offline oracle only to measure headroom, never as an online competitor.

**Predeclared rejection test:** stop this direction if cursor-based history plus a tuned adaptive continuation baseline meets the contract and the joint policy has no robust Pareto improvement in writes/energy versus latency/backlog across unseen traces and plausible cost ratios. Stop also if gains require free persistent input storage, free receiver commits, arbitrary changing energy capacities, oracle future energy, weakened exactness, or comparison only with oversized tasks. A fixed-size sweep matching the candidate is evidence against needing a new adaptive method. One counterexample to correctness rejects the protocol irrespective of average speedup. These are proposed decision rules, not measured results.

## Simulator credibility, effort and access limits

A simulator-only contribution can credibly establish invariants, modeled tradeoffs and failure counterexamples. It cannot establish real harvested-energy savings, analog brownout timing, FRAM/flash atomicity or sensor capture performance without validated hardware parameters. The current Cortex-M0 cycle harness can remain the arithmetic oracle and supply compute costs, but an explicit NVM/commit/reboot model and sensitivity analysis are new work. Do not transfer TI LEA/FRAM results directly to Cortex-M0 flash, or equate cycle reduction with energy reduction. The cited SONIC/TAILS and Samoyed evaluations use actual intermittent hardware; simulated reproduction should state its narrower evidence.

Expansion cost is high relative to an arithmetic selector: first specify and validate persistence/stream semantics, then build deterministic power-failure injection and recovery, then implement strong prior-art baselines, only then test a joint policy. This also needs a reason the target stores/replays its input while the processor loses power; otherwise the application premise fails. Without a distinct state-retention result, the deliverable is a useful simulator/reproduction study, not the requested original FIR method.
