## Three separate verdicts

| Question | Verdict as of this date | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Can this become a technically sound college project and empirical paper? | **Yes; feasibility is strong.** | Exact kernels, range certificates, compiled-output checks, saved event evidence, resource diagnostics and comparison infrastructure exist. The remaining central experiment is four fixed configurations, not an open-ended optimizer. Simulator-only evaluation fits the stated scope when claims remain model-specific. |
| Is there a valid research question or gap? | **A narrow empirical question is valid and still unanswered; a substantial field-level gap is not established.** | We need to know whether the apparent representation benefit survives comparable arithmetic specialization of direct accumulation, and what width, sharing and emission separately contribute. Published methods do not themselves determine that pinned comparison. Its practical importance and resulting lesson still depend on the peer result and explanation. |
| Has the project demonstrated algorithmic novelty or a publication-level contribution? | **No.** | Transposition, constant sharing, word-length/range analysis, local accumulation and architecture-sensitive code generation have close precedents. New measurements can constitute original empirical work, but a new target or a large benchmark alone does not establish meaningful field novelty. |

These are research judgments, not guarantees about grading or publication. The sensible commitment is to complete the existing bounded comparison and then choose an honest empirical claim. It is premature to commit to “a new energy-efficient FIR algorithm” or “transposed sharing beats direct FIR.”

## The defensible empirical gap

The question should be stated as:

> For the frozen integer FIR computation on the pinned Cortex-M0 setup, how do equally justified arithmetic widths, direct/transposed state organization and constant-graph emission affect complete finite-stream instruction cost, modeled CPU energy and memory resources?

This question is experimentally answerable and has a practical motivation: selecting a filter implementation from its final accumulator width or graph-operation count can miss costs introduced elsewhere. The literature already recognizes those general mechanisms. The potentially original contribution is a controlled explanation of their combined effect in the stated exact software setting, including a fair direct comparator and negative cases. That is an **inference about a contribution opportunity**, not an author-supported assertion that the field has neglected it.

Three distinctions matter. An unresolved result in our repository is a local evidence gap. A result absent from the primary sources inspected is a candidate literature gap. A meaningful field contribution additionally requires a useful lesson that survives stronger controls and is distinct from simply remeasuring an established implementation effect. At present, only the first is certain. A targeted search cannot establish firstness, and the professor may reasonably accept a careful empirical study without requiring a new algorithm.

## What simulator-only evidence can justify

The energy source is Nikov et al., [*Accurate Energy Modelling on the Cortex-M0 Processor for Profiling and Static Analysis*](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.12806v1), especially Table 2. The project selects 20 MHz, prefetch off and zero wait states. The paper's 2.80% MAPE for that configuration describes its own evaluation; it is neither a pairwise significance test nor an error bound for our local simulator or FIR rankings. The project's independently specified Arm-table timing estimate also assumes zero wait states and single-cycle multiplication. Physical board measurements are unnecessary for the stated college scope, but physical energy or wall-clock performance claims would exceed the evidence.

The current streams contain 257 samples with zero initial history. Especially for the 192-tap filter, that boundary represents a substantial startup interval; the findings support the measured finite-stream experiment, not a steady-state application claim. The filters are application-motivated synthetic designs, and changing the admitted input guarantee while reusing one actual ADC stream does not produce independent application workloads. General arithmetic proofs cover the admitted domain; timed examples do not thereby become representative of that domain. These are scope limits, not requests to expand the benchmark now. [Local protocol and findings](fir-sharing-findings.md).

## Bounded decision gate and contingencies

Complete the already agreed [missing-baseline plan](../docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-20-fir-baseline-check.md): one filter, full-int16 guarantee, fixed groups `[0,2)`/`[2,48)`, GCC10/GCC15, blocks 1/64, `-O2`, no LTO, the frozen inputs and existing measurement boundary. Do not add a partition search, more filters or an optimizer to make a favorable result appear.

1. **Admit the peer evidence before ranking it.** Match hashes, compiler/flags, numeric contract and measurement region. Check exact outputs, history, guards, raw counters and reconstructed costs independently. Inspect C arithmetic and assembly: widening must precede final addition, signed quantization must match the reference, and native-width partial accumulation must actually be emitted. Adapt a differing peer artifact layout explicitly; current checker completion is not unseen-peer certification.
2. **If a useful transposed advantage survives:** report the four configurations and code/frame/state tradeoffs, then explain the residual difference after direct narrowing. A bounded case-study contribution is plausible. “Existing representation and sharing choices can remain worthwhile after equal arithmetic specialization” is defensible only for configurations that demonstrate it; algorithmic novelty still does not follow.
3. **If partial direct removes the headline advantage:** revise the central result to the corrected comparison and baseline sensitivity. Preserve old results with their actual comparators. The investigation can still be valid college research if it explains a consequential mistaken inference and shows the corrected resource tradeoff. Merely discovering a weak baseline is not automatically enough for a strong paper.
4. **If the result is mixed or nearly tied:** report conditional behavior without a universal winner. A 0.17% modeled difference, for example, should not become an energy-saving headline. Prefer explanation and resource costs over a forced win count.
5. **If only routine, already understood effects remain:** retain the reproducible artifact and present it as a rigorous engineering/empirical project. Match the final presentation to the assignment requirements, treating the professor as final evaluator rather than an available project guide. If a new algorithm is required, this evidence remains insufficient; do not promise publication or expand the experiment merely to manufacture novelty.

No favorable percentage is required for this gate to succeed. Its success criterion is a trustworthy answer to the fixed comparison. The remaining research judgment is whether the corrected result yields a useful, properly bounded lesson.

This was a targeted research reassessment, not a systematic review, citation-complete search, formal arithmetic/code proof or patent/priority clearance. No absence-based first-of-kind claim follows. The strongest conclusion remains: **finish the small fairness correction; keep the project as a conditional empirical study; do not sell the existing techniques as a new FIR algorithm.**
