# Critical originality audit: current FIR arithmetic-contract study

19 October 2026. Independent assessment of the **current empirical project**, not the discarded quantization-skipping proposal. FIR only; simulator only; college research paper, publication optional.

## Decision

**Continue, but narrow the claim. The experimental foundation is worthwhile; the current result set does not yet establish a meaningful new research contribution.** This is a suitable college research direction if the next stage produces reusable, falsifiable implementation guidance. Presenting “smaller input range permits faster 32-bit FIR” or “folded C can compile badly” as a new algorithm would overstate originality.

The defensible target is a **correctness-aware empirical FIR/compiler study**: characterize where arithmetic contracts alter the valid implementation set, explain generated-code costs, and establish whether a compact selection rule transfers to previously unseen FIR configurations. A new measurement table or automatic selector alone would not secure novelty. This judgment is an inference from the project evidence and primary literature below, not a claim that every related paper has been exhausted.

## What this audit actually checked

Read the local [proposal](../professor-topic-proposal.md), [baseline findings](fir-baseline-findings.md), and [direction screen](fir-next-direction-screen.md). The stated 486 binary runs and 124,902 comparisons were not rerun or independently reconstructed in this audit. They support correctness checks of the artifact, not the originality claim. The effective filter diversity remains six symmetric filters in two design families; repeating reference positions under compiler/block settings does not create hundreds of independent application filters.

Targeted primary-source searches covered FIR fixed-point range analysis, exact/bit-exact word-length specialization, processor-aware code generation, and compiler/resource characterization. Abstract-only evidence is distinguished below. An exact duplicate of the entire proposed experiment was not identified; this negative search result is weak evidence and is **not** the basis for recommending continuation.

## What the existing results mean for novelty

**Same samples, different guaranteed domains.** This is a sound way to isolate the value of static information: shared eligible binaries receive the same stream, while an additional valid narrow candidate is admitted under the smaller domain. However, the causal expectation is straightforward: if a wide operation is expensive and the range proves it unnecessary, narrower code can be cheaper. The 22.74% modeled difference is a useful quantified case, not a surprising general principle. The result concerns one filter family/length crossing an admission boundary in the present bank; six block/flag replications do not make six independent mathematical discoveries. [Local result and exact arithmetic values](fir-baseline-findings.md).

**Folded source code can perform more machine multiplies.** The six-MUL wide helper explains the observed outcome on this pinned compiler. This is valuable causal diagnosis, but a general wide-expression control is not the strongest baseline. Product-certified folding and CMSIS fast already address much of the avoidable cost. An old compiler-specific missed opportunity is weak research novelty unless shown to persist in relevant tools or turned into a specific, reusable finding. [Local disassembly-based finding](fir-baseline-findings.md).

**Exact arithmetic and complete streaming cost improve validity.** They prevent claiming gains from accidental overflow or omitted state copying. Those are necessary methodological strengths, not independently a new research question. Likewise, discovering and documenting simulator timing discrepancies improves trust but does not make the FIR optimization novel. [Local verification and simulator audit](fir-baseline-findings.md).

## The concrete contribution worth pursuing

Use the existing fixture to answer a narrower question:

> Which simple arithmetic and generated-code facts predict the cheapest valid exact Q15 FIR implementation on a stated small-core toolchain, and where do those predictions fail?

The desired research output is a **validated selection map with explanations and failure cases**, rather than a claim to invent narrowing. Proposed dimensions are guaranteed signed range, actual coefficient bounds, symmetry, required pre-add/product/accumulator widths, block/state cost, and compiler lowering. Do not merely fit a classifier to the six existing filters; a table lookup would reproduce the dataset without teaching anything reusable.

1. **Write the rule before its final evaluation.** Use established arithmetic to compute admission; use matched controls to attribute width/helper/state costs. Express a compact, inspectable selection policy. If the best policy turns out to be “choose fold32 whenever legal,” report that simplicity honestly; it may limit the significance of the work.
2. **Test on held-out, application-justified FIR designs and ranges.** Include examples on both sides of genuine arithmetic boundaries and non-winners, without modifying coefficients solely to manufacture gains. Specify ADC packing/scaling: 11-bit information stored after a left shift to Q15 does not retain the unshifted magnitude guarantee.
3. **Check modern relevance with one newer supported compiler and justified library build settings.** Compare against the strongest legal implemented candidate, including certified products and production fast paths. This is a targeted persistence check, not an open-ended flag sweep.
4. **Report selection regret and explanatory accuracy.** On unseen cases, compare the chosen implementation with the exhaustive best valid candidate from the declared set. Explain failures through event/code evidence. A correct safety checker plus a poor cost rule is an informative negative result; do not hide it behind average gains.
5. **Keep energy secondary to verified execution evidence.** Report model estimates with their assumptions, and separate robust instruction/cost reductions from close model-dependent rankings. No arbitrary percentage threshold or source-model MAPE proves physical significance.

None of these additions guarantees novelty. Their purpose is to turn an obvious optimization principle into specific new evidence that another embedded FIR implementer could use and that the closest papers do not already supply. The research claim must describe the actual final finding, not just the planned evaluation method.

## Go/no-go standard and professor wording

**Proceed for the college project**, with this contribution target and a bounded validation stage. Do not discard the verified baseline or restart topic hunting yet. If newer-tool checks and held-out cases only repeat the obvious “32-bit is cheaper when valid” story, the honest outcome is a strong engineering/replication project with limited field novelty. At that point, do not keep adding filters to make it look original; either accept that scope with the professor or identify a real unresolved compiler/selection failure before proposing an algorithm contribution.

Suggested wording:

> “Our pilot demonstrates contract-dependent implementation changes in exact Q15 FIR software. Range analysis and symmetry are established. We are testing whether their interaction with compiled product and accumulator widths yields a reusable implementation-selection rule, supported by held-out FIR cases and current-toolchain checks. Our present energy numbers are model estimates.”

**Originality status:** new local experimental work, yes; demonstrated novel algorithm, no; meaningful original empirical contribution, plausible but not yet established. This is the distinction to retain in the proposal and final paper.

## Follow-up after bounded validation, 19 October 2026

**Current decision: the stage is complete and supports a defensible college empirical research paper, but it has not established substantial field novelty or a new optimization method.** Do not expand the grid merely to postpone that judgment. The new result is a useful, rigorously qualified characterization of the limits of a simple FIR selection heuristic; the dominant explanations currently remain established library unrolling, state-copy and compiler effects.

The [fixed protocol](fir-validation-protocol.md) specifies the policy before new target results. The saved [analysis](fir-baseline/selection-analysis.json) records 522 binary executions, 267,786 exact output comparisons, 108 comparison groups, 762 admitted rows and 210 exclusions. The frozen policy selects the modeled-energy winner in 80/108 groups, with maximum regret 51.83%; within symmetric cases it succeeds in 80/90. Its nonsymmetric direct fallback loses all 18 groups to an unrolled CMSIS implementation. Compiler changes alter the winner in 10/54 matched groups. These are descriptive counts for six new author-designed filters and repeated conditions, not population estimates or independent samples of 108 applications. This audit inspected the protocol and saved analysis; it did not rerun target binaries.

This is stronger evidence than the pilot because it tests a declared prediction against additional structures and a newer compiler. Nevertheless, the failed prediction is not itself a novel discovery: choosing a straightforward scalar fallback instead of an available tuned library is an intentionally incomplete performance heuristic. The result demonstrates that arithmetic legality and source product counts alone do not choose the fastest/lowest-model-cost member of this candidate set. It does not demonstrate an unresolved compiler defect or a previously unknown FIR mechanism.

The primary source check explains why the observed fixes are not new. CMSIS-DSP v1.14.4 [standard Q15 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARM-software/CMSIS-DSP/v1.14.4/Source/FilteringFunctions/arm_fir_q15.c) and [fast Q15 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARM-software/CMSIS-DSP/v1.14.4/Source/FilteringFunctions/arm_fir_fast_q15.c) already implement optional four-output unrolling and unrolled history copying. The [official FIR documentation](https://arm-software.github.io/CMSIS-DSP/v1.14.4/group__FIR.html) discusses block-state storage, avoiding circular addressing and numeric overflow contracts. These implementation choices predate our experiment. SPIRAL's [author publication index](https://spiral.ece.cmu.edu/pub-spiral/allabstract.jsp), including the 2003 FIR study cited above, already positions platform-adapted FIR implementations and blocking as important beyond arithmetic counts. The present measurements quantify a particular interaction; they do not overturn that established understanding.

One baseline limitation needs explicit correction in interpretation: **odd-length FIRs are not fundamentally unsupported by CMSIS.** Its v1.14.4 documentation gives the workaround of adding a zero coefficient to obtain an even tap count. The protocol's exclusion of the 65-tap case is transparent, but its 18 policy wins therefore cannot establish an advantage over the strongest practical library baseline. No new target runs are required to acknowledge this limitation; exclude any “odd-length library gap” claim.

The policy evaluation is held out from pilot development. Explanations identified after examining the new code/events are **post hoc causal analyses**, not independently validated predictions. Revising the policy using these outcomes and scoring it on the same matrix would be training-set evaluation, not an additional successful transfer result.

Timing and modeled-energy winners differ in 33/108 groups, but the modeled-energy penalty of choosing the timing winner is only approximately 0.020–1.825%. This does not justify a physical time–energy proxy-failure claim: there is no pairwise physical uncertainty bound establishing that such close model rankings are reliable. Retain the observation as model-sensitive descriptive evidence.

Concrete professor claim:

> “We completed a reproducible, exact-output FIR study and tested a fixed arithmetic-based selection rule on six additional designs and two compilers. The rule often selects the lowest modeled-energy implementation, but known library and state-management effects cause important failures. Our contribution is a controlled empirical characterization and an openly reproducible artifact; we are not claiming a new FIR algorithm or measured hardware energy savings.”

For the stated college goal, this is worth presenting and writing up with its negative result and boundaries. If the professor requires a clearly new algorithm or a demonstrated unresolved field-level issue, **the current evidence does not meet that stronger requirement**. A selector/tool, more filters, or retuning the existing heuristic would not by themselves repair that novelty gap.

### Root-agent baseline correction after this follow-up

The documented odd-filter workaround was subsequently executed as a separate, post-evaluation [padding supplement](fir-baseline/selection-padded-cmsis.json), retaining the original policy/protocol/results. Its 48 additional binaries yield 24,624 matching outputs with correct logical history and canaries; combined totals are 570 binaries and 292,410 output comparisons. Against this stronger candidate set, policy energy agreement is **77/108** (mean regret 5.25%, worst 51.83%); odd-filter agreement is 15/18 rather than 18/18. Timing/model-energy winners differ in 39/108 groups, with at most about 2.001% energy penalty for the timing winner. These corrections strengthen baseline fairness without changing the originality verdict. [Detailed findings](fir-selection-validation-findings.md).
