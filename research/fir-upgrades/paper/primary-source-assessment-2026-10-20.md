## Overall judgment

**My assessment is approximately 7/10 as a research manuscript in its stated, bounded empirical category.** The work is technically serious and unusually careful about comparator controls, exact semantics, adverse outcomes and reproducible saved evidence. Its main contribution is a well-supported correction to an implementation comparison, followed by a useful generated-code investigation. It has limited algorithmic novelty and limited evidence of generality. These are evaluative judgments, not venue scores or acceptance predictions.

I would rate the quality of the engineering/project work higher, approximately **8–8.5/10**, while rating demonstrated conceptual/algorithmic novelty around **4–5/10**. Combining these dimensions into one score hides the principal tradeoff: substantial care and evidence support a fairly narrow new empirical result.

This assessment independently checked primary literature and official sources against the manuscript. It does not certify every raw artifact, rerun target measurements, establish historical priority, or identify a suitable publication venue.

## What is strongest

The coordinator also freshly ran `panel_verify.py --root research/fir-upgrades --correctness-only --output /tmp/fir-rating-review/correctness-audit.json`: exit status zero, 70 admitted correctness identities, two unavailable, and zero failed. No target execution was involved.

1. **The arithmetic correction is consequential.** Section 3.2 gives explicit full-domain group bounds and widens each native partial before the final addition. Section 5.1 reports that this stronger control overturns two original implementation choices. This is a meaningful experimental lesson: a winner over a needlessly wide direct accumulator need not win against direct code supplied with the same range knowledge. The paper correctly does not present the partition as a new arithmetic technique or as an optimal implementation.
2. **The controls separate two otherwise confounded questions.** Hcub versus ordinary transposition isolates incremental product sharing while fixing suffix-state equations; Hcub versus corrected direct answers a broader implementation choice involving state representation too. Sections 3.2, 5.2 and 7.2 distinguish these clearly. The finding that Hcub loses to ordinary transposition in all 32 fresh wide-design cells is scientifically valuable negative evidence, particularly because historical library-relative wins could have concealed it.
3. **The compiler intervention adds more than an assembly anecdote.** Sections 6.1–6.2 identify a value-flow account, reproduce historical general-kernel controls, and then expose literal block-one knowledge inside the callee. The matched intervention supports an effect of that source-level knowledge on generated code and modeled cost. Its explicit refusal to assign the entire saving to one removed transfer pattern is appropriate.
4. **The evidence has a sensible hierarchy.** Sections 4.2 and 8.3 distinguish arithmetic legality, finite correctness checks, saved-byte identity, regeneration, rebuild and physical replication. They also retain missing library cases and describe the retrospective/prospective chronology. This is stronger than simply reporting output agreement and a percentage speedup.

## Energy and target-model assessment

The six numerical weights and the 2.80% MAPE in §4.1 match Nikov et al.'s Table 2 configuration **[20 MHz, prefetch OFF, zero wait states]**. Their model is fitted to physical STM32F0 measurements using simulated counters. Section 2.3 says it omits an intercept and absorbs residual into event weights; §2.5 explicitly explains that the RAM-read coefficient partly reflects event frequency and regression attribution. Thus the paper is right to avoid treating weights as separately measured physical event prices. The source validates a configuration-specific model across its own benchmark suite, not this paper's particular FIR pairwise margins. [Nikov et al., §§2.1–2.5 and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.12806v1).

My resulting methodological judgment is that the modeled ranking calculations are a legitimate object of study, but the closest margins need physical confirmation before becoming deployment advice. Aggregate MAPE is neither a confidence interval nor a rule that every smaller difference is false. Equally, exact decimal arithmetic on event counts does not establish physical precision. The manuscript explains this correctly; the abstract's six-decimal nJ values still distract from that distinction.

Arm's zero-wait Cortex-M0 instruction summary lists MULS as **1 or 32 cycles**, depending on the implementation, and gives transfer/branch timings separately. Therefore the stated single-cycle-MUL assumption matters materially and cannot be generalized to every Cortex-M0 implementation. Add an explicit official Arm citation near the cycle equation, since the current reference list does not include the table used to justify it. [Arm Cortex-M0 Technical Reference Manual, instruction summary](https://support.arm.com/documentation/ddi0432/c/programmers-model/instruction-set-summary?lang=en).

The RAM-weight multiplier is useful algebraic sensitivity analysis. Because it changes two fitted coefficients together while holding executions fixed, it does not represent calibrated uncertainty, another device or another memory configuration. Sections 4.3 and 8.2 already state this. I would retain the analysis as a diagnostic and give it less headline weight than the actual controlled comparison and intervention.

## Comparator fairness and library concerns

Official CMSIS guidance distinguishes standard Q15 FIR accumulation from the fast Q15 path's 32-bit accumulator, whose overflow wraps; it recommends input scaling as a generic safeguard. Full-domain coefficient-specific range proofs can be a stronger eligibility test than that generic scaling rule, provided actual evaluation semantics are also admitted. The pinned v1.14.4 fast implementation contains architecture-specific paths and pointer-access helpers, so current overview documentation alone is not enough to certify a particular build. [CMSIS FIR documentation](https://arm-software.github.io/CMSIS-DSP/main/group__FIR.html); [pinned v1.14.4 fast-Q15 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ARM-software/CMSIS-DSP/v1.14.4/Source/FilteringFunctions/arm_fir_fast_q15.c).

The manuscript is responsible to leave unresolved native fast-CMSIS/block64 paths unavailable (§7.1), rather than silently count them as slower. However, that missing comparator still weakens the complete-panel and all-form conclusions. The source-level reason should remain specific: **unresolved pointer-object aliasing under this frozen admission policy**, not an established CMSIS defect or a proof that the path is unusable.

“Arithmetic-fair” is a useful local description, but it should not imply globally fair optimality. The candidate space still fixes schedules, two direct cuts, one Hcub graph seed, a narrow compiler set and build flags. Circular-buffer direct kernels, different partitions, whole-program optimization or another constant-multiplication schedule could alter the finite winners. These are possible further controls, not demonstrated flaws in the saved comparisons. Section 8.2 already admits the finite search space.

## Principal limitations and presentation concerns

- **The fresh design breadth is two widening-required coefficient arrays**, both 48 taps. The 32 wide cells are repeated compiler/block/amplitude/region conditions of those arrays; four conditions within each design/compiler/block group retain the same ranking. The paper disclaims independent statistical samples, but the abstract's 24/32 still visually suggests greater design breadth than is present. State “both fresh widening-required arrays” early and keep the count as supporting detail.
- **The prospective test is existential and relatively modest.** Demonstrating at least one fresh reversal is valid evidence of a counterexample boundary. It does not estimate how often comparator correction matters in real filter workloads, and the paper correctly avoids that inference.
- **The paper's strongest new result is empirical rather than algorithmic.** Reviewers expecting a new optimizer, synthesis algorithm or general decision rule will likely find the contribution too narrow. Readers interested in measurement methodology, software DSP or reproducible optimization case studies may find it worthwhile. This is an audience judgment, not a venue recommendation.
- **The research narrative is overburdened with process bookkeeping.** The original/fresh/intervention distinction is useful. Labels U1–U5, source-retrieval history in §2, exact package file counts and repeated audit-admission wording are less useful in the main argument. Move much of that detail to artifact documentation, then spend the recovered space on why a practitioner should care and which experiment isolates which effect.
- **Use fewer significant digits in prose and the abstract.** Retain exact values in the artifact and exact sensitivity calculations, but present the compiler-gap reversal and ordinary percentage changes at a readable precision. More decimals do not strengthen a physical claim.
- **Avoid apparent external validation.** Appendix B says the panel results passed “independent scientific review.” If this denotes internal agent/reviewer checks, label it explicitly as such; it could otherwise be mistaken for external peer review. The clean-copy audit establishes saved-evidence portability, not publication validation.

The pinned SciPy citation is sound: its documentation says homomorphic `half=True` produces approximately half the taps and approximates the square root of the original magnitude response. The paper correctly warns that these controls are synthetic rather than unchanged application filters. [SciPy 1.15.3 `minimum_phase`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.15.3/reference/generated/scipy.signal.minimum_phase.html).

## Highest-value next steps

1. Tighten the manuscript around three findings: range-equitable controls reverse decisions; MCM's incremental benefit can disappear after compilation; callee-visible block knowledge explains a large local compiler difference.
2. Add the official Arm timing citation, prefer the institutional SPIRAL source where accessible, and move source-access chronology and internal review labels out of the research narrative.
3. If strengthening the scientific contribution rather than only polishing the current case, prioritize a larger, varied coefficient/length panel and matched board measurements of the key corrected comparisons. Those answer the largest remaining questions of breadth and practical energy relevance.

I would encourage continuing the work, with its current modest claims intact. It is a credible and careful empirical paper with a clear lesson; it is not yet broad evidence for FIR implementation selection or a major algorithmic advance.
