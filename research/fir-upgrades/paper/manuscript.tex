\begin{abstract}

Exact FIR comparisons depend on the arithmetic-range information
supplied to each implementation. We study this dependence using
established direct and transposed realizations on a pinned Cortex-M0
simulator and CPU energy model. Replacing wide direct accumulation with
two certified int32 partial sums overturns two of four original
advantages of Hcub, a transposed implementation with shared constant
products. A panel frozen before performance evaluation extends this
finding to two fresh wide 48-tap arrays: stronger direct controls
reverse Hcub's advantage in 24 of 32 complete conditions. Yet Hcub loses
to ordinary transposition in all 32, separating product sharing from
realization choice. Eight unavailable library cases leave the full
three-array panel incomplete. Saved events expose the tradeoff between
arithmetic savings and RAM traffic. A matched direct-kernel intervention
makes a one-output bound visible inside the callee, removes identified
preparation for another output, and lowers GCC15 modeled cost by
31.44\%, reversing the compiler ranking for that pair. These findings
support a bounded empirical case study of arithmetic controls, state
representation and compiler knowledge, without a new FIR algorithm,
general selection rule or physical-energy advantage.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

An exact FIR comparison should give each implementation access to the
same arithmetic-range information. On a processor with native 32-bit
arithmetic, a full convolution sum may require 64 bits even when
separately accumulated groups fit in 32 bits. Comparing a transposed
implementation that exploits these bounds with a direct implementation
that uses wide arithmetic throughout can therefore confound the
realization with the arithmetic treatment.

Product sharing introduces a second distinction. Transposition exposes
the products of one input sample with many constants, allowing common
shift/add expressions to be reused. It also replaces input history with
weighted suffix states, changing their widths and lifetimes. A
library-relative advantage can therefore combine sharing, state
representation and other implementation choices. Ordinary transposition,
with the same state equations but unshared products, provides the
control needed to isolate sharing.

We examine these distinctions on a pinned Cortex-M0 simulator using a
configuration-specific CPU energy model. The original nonsymmetric
48-tap filter, \texttt{speech\_minphase48}, supplies four GCC10/GCC15
and block1/block64 comparisons. Two certified native partial sums make
direct accumulation less costly than Hcub under GCC10; Hcub remains less
costly under GCC15. This compiler split motivates a matched intervention
that exposes a one-output bound inside the direct callee and examines
the resulting machine code.

A prospectively frozen panel tests whether the baseline effect extends
to fresh coefficient arrays. It includes one native-sum and two
widening-required 48-tap designs, with paired input amplitudes and
measured stream regions. The original comparison motivated this panel;
its four-cell reporting rule is retrospective. The new panel and
intervention were fixed before their target costs were observed (Section
4).

The contribution is a controlled empirical account of three effects:
arithmetic correction changes the direct/Hcub comparison; sharing loses
to ordinary transposition on both fresh wide arrays; and fixed-block
knowledge changes the direct compiler ranking. Exact event sensitivity
and portable saved evidence support these findings. Range analysis,
transposition, Hcub and specialization are established techniques; field
priority and a general implementation rule remain unestablished.

\section{Closest prior work}\label{closest-prior-work}

Range-aware DSP code generation is established. De Coster et al.~{[}1{]}
use magnitude information in compiled bit-true DSP simulation to avoid
unnecessary overflow handling. Ménard and Sentieys {[}6{]} select
software word lengths and instructions under a
signal-to-quantization-noise ratio constraint, accounting for arithmetic
and transfers. Our full-int16 exact-output contract differs from their
accuracy constraint; it does not make width selection a new technique.

FIR generation and energy-oriented implementation selection also precede
this study. Gačić et al.~{[}2{]} generate FIR software with SPIRAL and
show that blocking and computation order change runtime at equal
arithmetic count. D'Alberto et al.~{[}3{]} optimize fixed-point XScale
transforms for runtime and physically measured energy. These results
motivate examining compiled behavior rather than arithmetic count alone.
Their hardware measurements do not validate our Cortex-M0 model
estimates.

Hcub provides established product-sharing graphs, rather than an
executable energy objective. Voronenko and Püschel {[}4{]} discuss
software concerns beyond addition count and explicitly leave
register-pressure optimization outside their study. We compare Hcub with
ordinary transposition to test its incremental benefit after
compilation, separately from the effect of changing realization.

The cost analysis and specialization use established tools. Nikov et
al.~{[}5{]} supply the configuration-specific event weights; our affine
reweighting is standard sensitivity analysis. GCC documents constant
propagation, cloning and interprocedural optimization {[}7{]}, but those
documents cannot identify the pass responsible for our historical
binaries. Saved-evidence portability follows established artifact
practice {[}10{]}. The contribution is the controlled empirical finding,
not these techniques. This targeted comparison is not an exhaustive
priority search.

\section{Exact contract and controlled representations}\label{exact-contract-and-controlled-representations}

\subsection{Numeric semantics}\label{numeric-semantics}

All filters have 48 frozen signed-int16 coefficients, ordered so that
\(h[0]\) multiplies the newest sample. Every candidate must accept all
\(x\in[-32768,32767]\), including −32768, regardless of the amplitude
used for performance measurement. Initial history is zero. For requested
samples only,

\[
\begin{aligned}
S[n]&=\sum_{k=0}^{47}h[k]x[n-k],\\
Q(s)&=\min\{32767,\max\{-32768,\lfloor s/32768\rfloor\}\},\\
y[n]&=Q(S[n]),\qquad x[n]=0\text{ for }n<0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Exactly one final quantization and saturation is permitted. There is no
intermediate truncation, saturation or appended convolution tail. In
particular, \(Q(-1)=-1\); truncation toward zero at that boundary
changes the contract. The generated quantizer clamps before narrowing
and implements negative floor using C division without assuming
arithmetic signed right shift.

For each coefficient, exact independent-input product endpoints are
\(l_k=\min(-32768h_k,32767h_k)\) and \(u_k=\max(-32768h_k,32767h_k)\).
Summing those endpoints gives tight intervals for the full sum and any
specified group. A sign-aligned legal sample window attains each
endpoint. A sufficient group-prefix certificate also uses
\(32768\sum_{k\in G}|h_k|\le\mathrm{INT32\_MAX}\): every product and
accumulated prefix within \(G\) fits int32. Full intervals, prefix
bounds, transposed suffix bounds and Hcub primitive/restored-product
identities are separate certificates. C evaluation widths must respect
them; widening after an overflowing addition is too late.

\subsection{The original arithmetic correction}\label{the-original-arithmetic-correction}

The correction uses native partial sums without changing the direct
history layout or coefficient order. The coefficient L1 norm is the sum
of absolute coefficient values. Fixed groups \([0,2)\) and \([2,48)\)
have coefficient L1 sums 12,778 and 59,731, hence absolute prefix bounds
418,709,504 and 1,957,265,408. Both fit int32. The full L1 sum is
72,509, with absolute bound 2,375,974,912; legal sign-aligned windows
attain 2,375,922,255 and −2,375,955,060, establishing that full-sum
int32 overflow is possible. Each completed partial is converted
separately before the int64 reduction:

\begin{Verbatim}[breaklines,breakanywhere,breaksymbolleft={},breaksymbolright={},fontsize=\scriptsize]
int64_t exact_sum = (int64_t)partial0 + (int64_t)partial1;
y[j] = quant(exact_sum);
\end{Verbatim}

This split was fixed for the arithmetic-control experiment. It
establishes legality, without claiming an optimal partition or direct
implementation.

Ordinary transposition computes the current sum from \(h[0]x+z_0^{old}\)
and updates \(z_i\) from \(h[i+1]x+z_{i+1}^{old}\), ending with the last
product. Ascending updates preserve the required old states. Suffix
bounds justify each state's width. Hcub adds independently checked
shared expressions and demand-driven emission near their consuming
stage. Safe exact operations replace problematic signed shifts.

The controls isolate different effects (Figure 1). Partial versus wide
direct changes the accumulation width while retaining input history.
Hcub versus ordinary transposition changes product sharing while
retaining suffix equations and layout. Hcub versus direct also changes
representation, and comparison with CMSIS changes further implementation
choices. Correctness checks reconstruct the intended history or suffix
values rather than compare unlike state bytes.

\section{Measurement, chronology and prospective questions}\label{measurement-chronology-and-prospective-questions}

\subsection{Target and event models}\label{target-and-event-models}

The target is Cortex-M0 Thumb at \texttt{-O2}, separate compilation and
no LTO. GCC10 reports 10.3.1 20210621; GCC15 is Arm GNU Toolchain
15.2.Rel1, reporting GCC 15.2.1 20251203. The simulator is pinned to
commit \texttt{\seqsplit{c55ea1b1926b792d7aabb7e3495b55a7b69ef134}}.
Code and coefficients reside in Flash, with state/input/output in SRAM.
Measurement includes dispatch, recurring calls, copies, convolution,
quantization, output stores and retained history/state movement,
including a final short call. Initialization, checking and printing lie
outside. The audited decoder removes the closing marker instruction
consistently.

Let \(N_I\) include all instructions, \(N_M\) native multiplication
instructions, \(B\) taken branches, \(R\) and \(W\) SRAM reads/writes,
\(F\) Flash data reads and \(N_{BL}\) branch-with-link instructions. The
zero-wait, single-cycle-MUL cycle estimate, using Arm's instruction
timings {[}11{]}, is

\[T=N_I+R+W+F+2B+N_{BL}.\]

Nikov et al.'s {[}5{]} 20 MHz, prefetch-off, zero-wait weights give
modeled CPU energy in nJ:

\[
\begin{aligned}
E={}&0.964258(N_I-N_M)+1.652455N_M\\
 &+2.091986B+1.109833R+0.650563W+0.633621F.
\end{aligned}
\]

The reported 2.80\% mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) describes
aggregate validation of that model, not uncertainty in our pairwise
margins. Fitted weights can absorb correlations and residual effects;
they are not separately measured physical event prices. We preserve
integer events and exact decimal products for reproducibility. The
0.000001 nJ tolerance used to read historical floating-point data
concerns serialization only.

\subsection{Correctness checks and study chronology}\label{correctness-checks-and-study-chronology}

The arithmetic correction is a retrospective control. The original
protocol covered three designs, two input guarantees, two blocks and two
compilers. Observed unequal width treatment motivated the fixed
two-group correction and then the compiler investigation. An earlier
source-level state-traffic estimate was withdrawn because register
retention and grouped transfers could reduce it; no candidate had been
excluded using that estimate. Previously inspected filters remain
development evidence. The fresh panel and intervention were frozen
before their target costs were observed.

The original comparison uses a saved 257-sample \texttt{adc\_raw} stream
with zero initial history. Block1 makes 257 calls; block64 makes four
full calls and a final one-sample call. Let \(r=100(E_H/E_P-1)\), where
\(H\) is Hcub and \(P\) is partial direct. The retrospective all-four
superiority claim, \(H_R\), requires \(r<0\) in every compiler/block
cell. A tie defeats strict superiority; missing or invalid evidence
leaves the assessment unavailable. This deterministic rule does not
involve significance testing.

Correctness admission combines checks that address different failure
modes. Arithmetic certificates establish full-domain source legality.
Finite UBSan checks and target output/state/guard checks test
implementation behavior. The original independent check covered 1,028
new, 6,168 historical and 722 signed-edge output comparisons.
Emitted-code inspection confirmed native partials and a wide final
reduction in all four corrected binaries. Hashes bind measured events to
checked sources and binaries. Library paths additionally require pinned
source/toolchain semantics; output agreement alone is insufficient.

\subsection{Saved-event analysis}\label{saved-event-analysis}

We use saved events to identify the work saved or added by each
comparison. For each compiler/block, the three oriented pairs are
partial minus wide direct, Hcub minus ordinary transposition and Hcub
minus partial direct. All use the same 257 outputs. With
\(v=(N_I-N_M,N_M,B,R,W,F)\) and the weights above,
\(\Delta E=w\cdot\Delta v\); negative favors the left form. Six-event
dominance requires all coordinates nonpositive with at least one
strictly negative; mixed signs preclude it. Resources are excluded.

A predeclared sensitivity check scales both RAM weights by \(\lambda\)
while holding the other four weights and all events fixed. For
\(\lambda\ge0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E(\lambda)&=A+B_{RAM}\lambda,\\
B_{RAM}&=1.109833\Delta R+0.650563\Delta W.
\end{aligned}
\]

We examine the arbitrary interval \([0.5,2]\) to locate algebraic
ranking boundaries. It is not calibrated to physical prices, model
uncertainty or another hardware configuration. Exact decimal products
give rational boundaries \(-A/B_{RAM}\); displayed roots are rounded.
Linearity makes endpoint signs sufficient to decide strict ordering over
the closed interval, with any tie defeating strictness. The artifact
retains all 12 vectors, components, resource deltas and root
classifications.

Two paired inputs test amplitude, and two measured regions test stream

regions differ in segment and length, this contrast does not isolate

A condition fixes the array, compiler, block size, input amplitude and
region; a performance identity additionally fixes the implementation.
The frozen inventory has 288 identities: 3 arrays × 6 forms × 2
compilers × 2 blocks × 2 amplitudes × 2 regions. Separate correctness
admission uses at most 72 target identities on a frozen 194-sample
zero/extremal/edge stream, plus host UBSan checks with mixed block
partitions. Images and conservative live call chains must fit 64 KiB
Flash/8 KiB RAM. Unavailable and failed cases remain explicit;
conditions are not independent statistical samples.

The primary question is whether a stronger direct comparator reverses an
old-control Hcub advantage. For a fixed-compiler condition, let
\(D_{old}\) be the minimum eligible nontransposed old control: wide
direct, standard CMSIS and fast CMSIS where eligible. Let \(D_{new}\)
add the available certified direct forms. A reversal satisfies
\(E_H<D_{old}\) but \(E_H\ge D_{new}\). The prospective extension
requires at least one such complete condition in either wide array. No
reversal among the complete wide conditions refutes that extension; a
missing required case leaves the full-panel assessment incomplete. All
ties retain every minimizer.

Additional comparisons distinguish implementation choices from that
reversal predicate. We report all-form winners, certified/wide and
Hcub/ordinary percentages, events, resources and paired input/region
contrasts. A separate compiler-selectable analysis minimizes each form
across compilers before comparing forms. Exact reference sums count the
clamp conditions \(S\ge1073741824\) and \(S\le-1073741824\) in each
measured region; output endpoints alone cannot identify those
conditions.

\subsection{Matched block-knowledge intervention}\label{matched-block-knowledge-intervention}

The intervention tests whether callee-visible block knowledge changes
the original direct compiler comparison. It uses the original block1
fixture and 257-sample stream, with each compiler building a general and
a specialized kernel. Specialization replaces only the three expression
uses of runtime \texttt{b} inside \texttt{probe\_process} with literal
\texttt{1} and adds \texttt{(void)b;}. Every call satisfies \(b=1\).
Arithmetic, groups, quantizer, history algorithm, wrapper, ABI, flags
and separate compilation without LTO remain fixed.

Historical comparability requires the general controls to reproduce the
saved loadable binaries and events exactly. Four signed-edge target
checks and repeated-one-sample host checks support correctness. Define
the signed compiler gaps \(G_g=E_{15,general}-E_{10,general}\) and
\(G_s=E_{15,specialized}-E_{10,specialized}\); the historical
\(G_g=70469.699925\) nJ. The predeclared success criterion requires
three observations: identified preparation for another output disappears
from reviewed code, GCC15 modeled cost falls, and \(G_s<G_g\). Event,
cycle and resource deltas document the full change. Attributing that
total to a particular transfer subset would require further accounting.

\section{Compiler account and intervention}\label{compiler-account-and-intervention}

\subsection{Explaining the original block1 binaries}\label{explaining-the-original-block1-binaries}

The original block1 compiler difference is largely a stack-traffic
difference. GCC15 executes 242,490 instructions, 43,182 RAM reads and
36,500 RAM writes; GCC10 executes 198,670, 29,304 and 17,225. Both use
two native partials and a wide final reduction, eleven native
MULs/output, nine saved/restored ABI words/call, and four stores/reloads
at the partial-to-wide interface. Extra multiplications or wide per-tap
arithmetic therefore do not explain the gap.

The binaries prepare differently for another output. GCC15 preloads
history and maintains a sliding sample window in registers and stack
slots, rotating samples before testing the output-loop bound. GCC10
loads mostly near use and carries one sample forward. For example,
GCC15's \texttt{{[}sp,\#40{]}} holds history sample 45, supplies
coefficient 12990, then receives sample 46 for a possible next
iteration. The callee remains general even though every call requests
one output. Stack residence alone does not prove a register-allocation
spill.

Checked paths reconcile the additional RAM traffic exactly. All 257
calls have \(b=1\); the copy-in and convolution loops iterate once. The
47-step history copy and alternative quantizer paths have no direct-SP
access, and the latter rejoin before the common tail. SP is fixed, there
are no internal calls, and GCC10's zero-block store is unreachable.
GCC10 executes 9 loads/9 stores relative to SP per call; GCC15 executes
63/84. The difference is 13,878 reads and 19,275 writes. This path-based
count supplements aggregate logs, which are not per-PC traces.

\section{Discussion and limits}\label{discussion-and-limits}

\subsection{What the comparisons establish}\label{what-the-comparisons-establish}

The central lesson is that the baseline and the comparison being
isolated can change the interpretation of a nominal winner. Correcting
direct arithmetic reverses two original Hcub decisions and 24 conditions
across the two fresh wide arrays. Yet Hcub loses to ordinary
transposition throughout those fresh wide conditions. Its advantage over
old direct/library controls therefore cannot establish a benefit from
product sharing.

Compiler knowledge supplies a second mechanism. Making the one-output
bound visible inside the direct callee removes identified preparation
for another output, lowers GCC15 cost and reverses the compiler ranking.
Other code changes accompany specialization, so the total saving cannot
be assigned exclusively to sample rotation or to a particular compiler
pass.

These findings support an empirical case study using established
techniques. Exact event tradeoffs and portable evidence make its
comparisons auditable; they do not add an optimization algorithm.
Compiler-dependent winners, tied partitions and unavailable native cases
also preclude a simple width-based selection rule.

\subsection{Internal and external validity}\label{internal-and-external-validity}

The evaluated implementations form a finite candidate set. Arithmetic
proofs, graph checks, UBSan, target output/state/guard checks, binary
identity and emitted-code inspection address distinct failure modes;
output agreement alone proves neither C safety nor model validity. The
fixed cuts, schedules, graph seed and build options are controls, not an
exhaustive optimization search. Freezing them before new costs prevents
outcome-driven replacement.

The fresh panel has limited design breadth: three synthetic arrays at
one length, including only two widening-required arrays. Repeated
conditions do not establish application prevalence. The original ADC
stream spans −1024 to 1023; \(1024\times72509=74249216\) bounds output
magnitude by 2266 without saturation. New full-range inputs exercise
source clamps, but remain finite stimuli. Full-domain arithmetic
legality does not make them exhaustive performance coverage, and the
post-startup region is not proven steady state.

The results depend on the pinned simulator and cost configuration. Arm
permits single-cycle or 32-cycle multipliers {[}11{]}; our cycle
estimate assumes the single-cycle implementation. Zero waits, frequency
and prefetch settings are also fixed. Board current, peripheral energy
and physical uncertainty are unmeasured. Reweighting fixed events does
not simulate another configuration, which could change execution itself.
The block1 intervention likewise establishes no block64 behavior or
ranking among other FIR forms.

Memory costs remain separate from modeled energy. Original direct state
is 98/224 bytes at blocks 1/64, versus 192 bytes for transposition.
Appendix A's process frames and selected symbols exclude complete
call-chain and image costs. The fresh panel additionally checks
linked-memory and conservative live-stack bounds. Summing these
different diagnostics would misstate RAM use.

\subsection{Evidence portability and reproduction levels}\label{evidence-portability-and-reproduction-levels}

Saved-evidence portability is distinct from a fresh target rebuild. The
saved 7,266-file package passed seven relocated verification commands
covering numerical regeneration, historical controls, compiler
explanation, event analysis, the panel and intervention. The checks had
zero failures, integration skips or target-tool executions. Explicit
copied roots prevented fallback to the original workspace. The package
predates this editorial revision; its manifest and check report remain
records of that snapshot.

Rebuilding requires separately acquired pinned GCC10/GCC15, simulator
and CMSIS/Hcub dependencies; executable toolchains are excluded from the
archive. Hashes establish byte consistency, not authenticity or physical
validity. The package supports analysis regeneration and saved-binary
verification, without claiming fresh performance replication, a public
archive or a DOI.

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

Equalizing arithmetic-range information changes exact FIR implementation
comparisons in this Cortex-M0 model case study. Certified native
partials reverse two original Hcub decisions and 24 of 32 complete
conditions across two fresh wide 48-tap arrays. Hcub nevertheless loses
to ordinary transposition in every fresh wide condition, separating the
baseline effect from incremental sharing. A matched block1
specialization also removes identified preparation for another output
and reverses the direct compiler ranking.

The practical lesson is to examine arithmetic treatment, state
representation and compiler-visible bounds together when interpreting an
implementation comparison. The finite synthetic panel, unavailable
native library cases and model-specific costs limit that lesson's reach.
Broader coefficient/length coverage and physical validation of the key
rankings are the next steps toward practical selection guidance.
