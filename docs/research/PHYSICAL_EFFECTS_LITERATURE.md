# Physical supply effects and the proposed energy-ranking study

Assessed 18 October 2026. This note investigates whether software-induced supply droop, regulator transients and their connection to energy are new research topics. It complements [LITERATURE_AUDIT.md](./LITERATURE_AUDIT.md) and [RESEARCH_GAP.md](./RESEARCH_GAP.md). Findings below distinguish inspected primary full texts, abstract-only evidence and our own deductions. This is a bounded literature search, not proof that an exact study is absent.

**Follow-up:** [NARROW_GAP_LITERATURE_CHECK.md](./NARROW_GAP_LITERATURE_CHECK.md) adds closer MCU/supply co-modeling precedents and tightens the candidate contribution. Supply-aware modeling, measured startup traces and conversion-dependent energy optima are established. The unresolved evaluation concerns the additional selection accuracy of a stateful model over a time-resolved steady-efficiency baseline, rather than merely over cycles.

## Assessment

**The broad effects and their connection to software and energy have already been studied.** Changing instruction schedules to mitigate droop predates this project; software-directed droop mitigation with architectural simulation and a manufacturer SPICE regulator model also predates it. Workload-dependent regulator efficiency and embedded voltage-transition overhead are established topics. The evidence table identifies what each source actually supports.

**A narrower question remains a candidate:** do supply-path dynamics change the physically measured energy ordering of bit-equivalent, nearly equal-cycle MCU implementations, and can a trace-based supply model predict that change on held-out configurations? The sources inspected here do not establish that exact STM32F051/FIR comparison. That observation does not establish novelty, feasibility, or that a reversal occurs.

## Terminology and causal interpretation

Voltage droop is a reduction in voltage, normally described as a magnitude such as millivolts or percentage of nominal voltage. `dV/dt` is voltage slew rate; `di/dt` is current slew rate. In a simplified inductive path, `v_L = L di/dt`; resistance contributes `v_R = R i`, and capacitance supplies transient current according to `i_C = C dV/dt`. These component equations describe different parts of a power-delivery network, rather than three interchangeable names for droop. The primary processor papers explicitly discuss current changes, parasitic supply impedance and resulting voltage excursions. [Gupta et al., introduction](https://vlsiarch.eecs.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum11281/files/vlsiarch/files/1283780.1283808.pdf), [Reddi et al., introduction and measurement methodology](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~simonec/files/Research/papers/RES_MICRO_2010.pdf)

Our interpretation: a droop waveform establishes a power-integrity effect; it does **not**, by itself, establish additional energy loss, its sign, or a software ranking reversal. Energy must be integrated at a stated boundary. An ideal inductor stores energy rather than dissipating it. A change in supply voltage can also change load behavior, so causality cannot be inferred by correlating peak droop with total energy alone.

## Package evidence and STM32 feasibility

**Package-dependent inductance is established.** ST's TN1163 §8 identifies reduced parasitic inductance from short WLCSP paths. ST's AN5346 §2.5.2 explicitly contrasts LQFP with lower-inductance UFBGA/UFQFPN/WLCSP in an ADC-reference context. Those documents establish an electrical mechanism, not an energy-ranking result for FIR or STM32F051. [ST TN1163](https://www.st.com/resource/en/technical_note/dm00103228-description-of-wlcsp-for-microcontrollers-and-recommendations-for-its-use-stmicroelectronics.pdf), [ST AN5346](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/dm00625282-stm32g4-adc-use-tips-and-recommendations-stmicroelectronics.pdf)

**Package labels do not specify the effective supply inductance.** TI's AN-1205 §§1–3 distinguishes partial, self and mutual inductance and provides example package RLC models. A supply/return loop and its interconnections must be modeled; an arbitrary per-pin inductance is not an extracted MCU PDN. The attachment's 5 nH versus 0.1 nH values have not been verified for the proposed STM32 parts. [TI AN-1205](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/snoa405a/snoa405a.pdf)

The STM32F051 family datasheet documents both LQFP48 and WLCSP36. It specifies external VDD at 2.0–3.6 V and describes an internal regulated 1.8 V domain. Sleep, Stop and Standby have different regulator/clock behavior (§§3.5.1–3.5.4); the pinout and supply scheme do not document an externally probeable core-regulator output. External VDD probing therefore must not be described as directly measuring core voltage. Package availability in a family does not prove the same die revision, process bin or internal layout across purchased parts. [STM32F051 datasheet, §§3.5, 4, 6.1.6 and 7](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/stm32f051t8.pdf)

Our experimental judgment: a two-package comparison would need matched operating settings, memory placement, GPIO states, PCB stackup/routing, decoupling, source impedance and temperature, plus multiple chips/boards per package. Even then, measured package-level association is not isolated inductance causation without electrical characterization or vendor models. A controlled supply-path change on one device is a simpler first causal experiment.

## Corrections to the attached methodology

The attachment is treated as a proposal to check, not evidence that its physical claims are true.

| Droop is `dV/dt`, described by `L di/dt + R i`. | Distinguish droop magnitude, voltage slew and current slew. That expression is a simplified series-path drop, not a complete regulator/decoupling model. |
| Standard instruction-level models assume constant power. | Too broad: instruction/event weights already represent activity-dependent energy. A fitted rail-energy model may implicitly include supply losses in its training conditions without modeling their dynamics explicitly. Reusing such coefficients plus added “LDO loss” risks counting the same energy twice. The modeling boundary must be checked. [Nikov et al., §§2.1–2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.12806v1) |
| Set “core voltage” to 1.8 V externally on STM32F051. | Confuses internal domain with specified external VDD; see platform evidence above. It is not a valid nominal supply setting for this device. |
| Voltage dips automatically insert extra Flash wait states. | Not supported by the documented device behavior. RM0091 §3.5.1 defines software-writable `FLASH_ACR.LATENCY` from SYSCLK: zero wait state up to 24 MHz and one up to 48 MHz. No droop-triggered adaptive wait-state mechanism was established. Treat errors/resets separately from valid completed executions. [ST RM0091](https://www.st.com/resource/en/reference_manual/dm00031936.pdf) |
| LDO recovery always takes 10–100 microseconds. | No device-specific basis supplied. Recovery depends on load step, capacitance, bias and regulator design. [ST AN5999](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an5999-how-to-use-stlinkv3pwr-to-monitor-power-consumption-on-stm32-mcus-and-mpus-stmicroelectronics.pdf) |
| Reduce 100 nF decoupling to 10 nF as a generic test. | Do not assume a value change is a controlled package experiment. Start with a documented stable network and characterize the altered impedance. AN4080 Fig. 1/§5.4 provides default decoupling guidance and notes application dependence. [ST AN4080](https://www.st.com/resource/en/application_note/an4080-getting-started-with-stm32f0x1x2x8-hardware-development-stmicroelectronics.pdf) |

The guide also uses two different counter numberings in its overview and coefficient table. Map weights by named events, and reconcile numbering before reusing its diagram or implementation.

## Why regulator losses do not automatically produce a new reversal

The following is our analytical deduction from the ordinary LDO efficiency relation, not a new result from an experiment. For constant input/output voltages, constant quiescent current, unchanged capacitor state, and an interval of duration `T`:

```text
E_source ≈ V_in ∫ I_load(t) dt + V_in I_q T
E_load   ≈ V_out ∫ I_load(t) dt
E_source ≈ (V_in / V_out) E_load + V_in I_q T
```

If quiescent overhead is negligible, the regulator simply scales load energy by a positive constant, preserving its ordering. If it matters, runtime-dependent overhead can change the comparison, but that is not evidence of a special transient penalty. A genuinely time-varying supply response needs measured or validated model parameters. The manufacturer's efficiency relation and discussion of quiescent current support the starting assumptions. [Analog Devices](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/analog-dialogue/articles/applying-low-dropout-regulators.html)

Energy conservation also requires separating dissipated loss from energy temporarily stored in capacitors/inductors:

```text
E_source − E_load = E_dissipated + ΔE_stored
```

This identity is our accounting guidance. For a capacitor, `E_C = ½ C V²`; ending the measurement while the supply is still recovering can move recharge energy outside the observed interval. Compare equal initial/final rail states, or include the storage correction and recovery tail. Do not label all input-minus-load energy measured in an arbitrary short window as regulator heat.

## Candidate study and useful discriminating tests

Proposed question, still requiring a fuller novelty search:

> Across bit-exact MCU kernel implementations with small execution-cycle differences, does the external supply path change their energy ordering relative to energy measured at the MCU supply pins? Can a calibrated trace-driven circuit model predict this ordering across held-out workloads and realistic regulator/decoupling configurations?

This endpoint is narrower than discovering droop, discovering LDO loss or proposing trace-driven SPICE for the first time. A null result is useful if it places a defensible bound on proxy error within a specified configuration. It should not promise to find reversals.

Recommended design choices below are our proposals, not literature findings:

1. Establish bit-exact computations and independently measure baseline energy before adding causal explanations.
2. Define whether the target is MCU-pin energy or battery/source energy. Measure both sides of an accessible external regulator over matched intervals to separate load and supply-path effects.
3. Compare one-shot bursts against repeated steady execution. Sweep burst duration and idle spacing to distinguish startup/recovery costs from sustained computation. Measure idle baselines under the same timing protocol.
4. Change one identified supply-path factor at a time: a documented regulator, approved decoupling range, or controlled series impedance. Keep software, voltages, frequency, temperature and instrumentation comparable.
5. Record energy, rail deviation and recovery time separately. Report peak droop and current slew as explanatory measurements, rather than substituting them for energy.
6. Validate the circuit model against load-step measurements before software-waveform predictions. A piecewise-current trace created from guessed per-instruction amplitudes is not independent validation.
7. Use paired repeats, uncertainty estimates and held-out configurations for ordering decisions. Account for shunt burden, bandwidth, channel timing alignment, board peripherals and equal storage states.

Package comparisons require an additional feasibility and confounding assessment: package name alone is not an inductance model, and different boards/die revisions/routing cannot be attributed exclusively to package inductance. Platform-specific source verification is a separate requirement before selecting that experiment.

Recommended starting scope, as our judgment: hold the MCU/package fixed and test burst cadence and an accessible external regulator first. Fit or calculate a time-resolved load-dependent steady-efficiency baseline with quiescent and startup costs, then ask whether validated supply dynamics explain a statistically resolved residual or improve ordering predictions. A higher-energy burst, by itself, does not isolate transient loss. If the expected difference is smaller than measurement uncertainty, report it as unresolved; do not force a reversal by operating outside the documented configuration.
